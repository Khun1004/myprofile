Growth & Passion

I was born and raised in Myanmar with no prior exposure to coding or computers — just a regular academic path. Coming to Korea was driven by a spirit of challenge and a search for new opportunities. During a year at a Korean language institute, I was exposed to a range of technical fields while learning the language and culture, and I found myself especially drawn to designing and building web pages — which naturally led me toward frontend development, where I could shape visual elements and user interfaces directly.

At Yeungjin College, in the Software/AI & Big Data track, I learned the fundamentals of web development systematically. Everything felt unfamiliar and difficult at first, but I found real satisfaction in understanding HTML's structure and dressing it up with CSS. Using JavaScript and React to bring static pages to life — building dynamic, interactive UI myself — is what pulled me deeper into frontend development. Adjusting the balance of a layout, the harmony of colors, the readability of typography — watching a web page come together felt like creating a piece of art.

I also gained mobile interface design experience through an Android development course. Arranging information effectively on a small screen and designing user flows around touch interaction had its own distinct appeal, and that experience became a solid foundation for adapting flexibly across devices and platforms. Approaching unfamiliar environments and technologies with curiosity rather than fear, building skills one step at a time — that background is exactly what gives me the confidence to keep learning and adapting in a fast-changing frontend landscape.

"Every single line of code should be written with the user in mind" — a philosophy I arrived at through the PongPongPuppy project.

Strengths & Weaknesses

Strengths I dive into, a weakness I'm refining through teamwork

01 Deep focus and diligence

I had no exposure to programming before coming to Korea, but once something catches my interest, I dig into it on my own. Tools like React and Git weren't covered in my regular coursework, but when a team project required them, I learned by working through online resources and official docs — which let me build React-based websites and even a React Native app on my own. I lose track of time getting hands-on with any technology and shipping a visible result, sometimes forgetting to eat while I'm deep in focus. That level of immersion and diligence is my biggest strength as a developer.

02 A feel for languages and cultures

Growing up in Myanmar and adapting to a new environment in Korea, I now use Korean confidently at Level 5. I can also communicate in English at an intermediate level, and I'm steadily learning Japanese and Thai from the basics. Learning and using multiple languages has given me a foundation for communicating smoothly with people from different cultural backgrounds — something I believe is valuable when designing user-centered services or collaborating across teams. I'm confident this sense for language and culture would be especially useful at a company operating global services.

03 Still growing — refining through collaboration

I tend to worry excessively about inconveniencing others. Afraid of making mistakes or being a burden, I often tried to solve everything on my own. But through repeated team projects and collaboration, I've come to appreciate the value of "doing it together," and I'm learning to speak up honestly and ask for help when I'm stuck. I can now voice my own opinions while still being mindful of the team's harmony, and my collaboration skills keep improving. Building on my ability to focus deeply and learn independently, I want to keep growing — turning this weakness into strength through better collaboration and communication, as a developer who has a positive impact on the team.

Career Goals

"A user-focused frontend engineer, designing experiences that win on the details."

I believe frontend development is about more than implementing features — it's about designing how technology shapes a user's emotions and behavior. Building PongPongPuppy taught me how much a single CSS pixel, a color choice, or a bit of whitespace can shape the overall user experience, and building New You sharpened my ability to weave complex features into an intuitive mobile flow. I want to bring that eye for detail and technical execution to a team where it can create real value.

1

A developer who sees things through

I don't stop at "it works" — I think through how a feature actually affects the people using it, and I raise the bar on polish. No bug, no layout glitch is too small to fix properly.

2

A teammate who grows with the team

I value the team's growth over my own individual wins. I don't insist on my own opinion — I prioritize the team's direction, and I want to build a culture of sharing knowledge and growing together.

3

An expert who turns learning into action

In a fast-moving industry, I stay committed to learning new tools and technologies — and to proving that learning through real gains in productivity and usability.

Short-Term Goals

Right after joining, my first priority is adapting quickly to the team's development culture and process. I'll learn to see my own code through a teammate's eyes via code review, and use tools like ESLint and Prettier to keep code consistent and high-quality. My first milestone is contributing to team productivity by deeply understanding the design system and carefully building reusable components. I'll also actively pick up CI/CD practices I haven't yet used in production, so I can get comfortable with the deployment process quickly.

Long-Term Goals

Longer term, I want to grow beyond implementation into a proactive engineer who analyzes user data and feedback to surface business problems and propose solutions. Drawing on my experience across web and mobile, I want to help deliver a consistent UX across platforms, and take the initiative on frontend architecture improvements that raise both development productivity and user satisfaction. Sparking technical discussion within the team and helping shape a better engineering culture — that's the kind of teammate I want to be.

The determination and adaptability I built while learning a new language and adjusting to a new culture in Korea are my greatest assets. They're the foundation for staying open to new technology and shifting requirements, and adapting with flexibility. I'll keep putting the user first, and do my best to be a teammate people can trust and rely on.
